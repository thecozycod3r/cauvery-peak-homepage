Grower to Connoisseur

From seed to cup, without leaving the estate.

Most coffee brands can tell you which region their beans came from. We can show you the ground they grew on, the terrace they dried on and the room they were roasted in.

  1. Nursery

    Seed is taken from mother plants selected on the estate for their cup, not their yield. It is raised here in the nursery until it is strong enough to go out into the field.

    Three years pass before a plant gives its first crop.

  2. Cultivation

    The coffee grows under two tiers of shade trees at 1,250 to 1,450 metres. Mist sits on the hills most mornings and slows the cherry down.

    Slower ripening is why the bean develops the flavour it does.

  3. Harvest

    Only fully ripe red cherry is taken, hand-picked across several selective rounds through the season rather than stripped in one pass.

    A single tree may be picked four or five times.

  4. Pulping

    Cherry is sorted and pulped the same day it comes off the tree. Nothing is left sitting overnight in fruit.

    Same-day pulping is the difference between clean and fermented-tasting coffee.

  5. Washing

    The pulped bean ferments overnight in tanks, then the mucilage is washed clean in channels.

    Washing is what gives the cup its clarity.

  6. Drying

    Parchment is spread on concrete terraces and sun-dried, turned by hand through the day, down to under 12% moisture.

    Dried too fast and the bean cracks. Too slow and it moulds.

  7. Milling & grading

    The remaining parchment skin is milled off. The green bean is then graded by size and density, and defects are picked out by hand.

    Hand sorting is slow. It is also the only way to catch what machines miss.

  8. Roasting

    We roast after your order is placed, in small batches, on the estate. Coffee is not roasted to sit in a warehouse waiting for a buyer.

    Roasted to order is the whole argument for buying from a grower.

  9. Packed & shipped

    Roasted coffee rests 24 hours to cool and degas, then it is packed and sent from the estate itself.

    No warehouse, no middleman, no repacking.

Why it matters

This is what single estate actually means.

Most coffee changes hands four or five times between the tree and your kitchen — grower, curer, exporter, importer, roaster. Every handover is a point where traceability is lost and where someone blends your lot with somebody else's.

Here there are no handovers. The estate that grew it is the estate that milled it, roasted it and packed it against your order. If a bag tastes wrong, we know exactly which block it came from.
