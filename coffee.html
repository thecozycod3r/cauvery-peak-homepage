The coffee

Same hill.
Three heights.
Three cups.

The only thing that really separates our three coffees is how high up the hill they grow. Here is what that does to the taste — in plain English, before any of the tasting words.

Shop Coffee Visit the Estate

The whole idea, in one line

Higher is brighter.
Lower is rounder.

Higher up it is colder, so the cherry ripens slowly and the cup comes out light and citrusy. Lower down it is warmer, the cherry ripens faster, and the cup comes out heavy, sweet and low on acidity. That is the entire difference between our three estates. Everything below is detail.

  • Shevaroys · 4,800 ft Coldest ground, bauxite soil, mountain springs. The cherry takes the longest to ripen here. Tastes bright and citrusy
  • Cauvery Peak · 4,600 ft The middle of the hill, and the Hawaiian Red Caturra seed brought back from Costa Rica in 1965. Tastes balanced and chocolatey
  • Glenfell · 4,100 ft Warmest and lowest, under heavy mixed shade, with clove and nutmeg growing between the coffee rows. Tastes round and faintly spiced
